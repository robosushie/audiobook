The teacup on Grandmother's shelf had a chip in the rim and a dragon asleep inside it. The dragon was the size of a spoon. Its scales were yellow, its wings were folded like napkins, and a thread of steam rose from its nose even though the cup was empty.

Dev found it on a Sunday when he was supposed to be dusting. He was seven. He froze with the cloth in his hand. The dragon opened one eye.

"Please do not pour tea on me," it said, in a voice like a kettle just before it sings. "I am having a dream about clouds."

"I was only dusting," Dev said.

"Dust is how I hide," said the dragon. "My name is Puff. I grant one small wish a week, and only if it helps someone else. Large wishes give me hiccups."

Dev thought of a bicycle, then of a mountain of sweets, then of his little sister Nia, who had lost her favorite button and had been quiet all morning. The button was blue, with a tiny star. Nia checked pockets the way sailors check the sky.

"Can you find a button?" Dev asked.

Puff climbed onto the saucer. "Finding is a small wish. Hold the cup and think of the person, not the thing."

Dev thought of Nia's laugh. The steam curled into a path across the kitchen, out the door, and toward the courtyard well. Dev followed with the cup in both hands. Puff rode the rim like a captain.

By the well, a crow sat on the rope. In its beak was a flash of blue. "That is not yours," Dev said, politely, because his grandmother said crows remember manners. He set down a handful of crushed biscuit. The crow considered the trade, dropped the button, and took the biscuit with professional pride.

Nia was on the steps. When she saw the button she made a sound that was half laugh and half relief. Dev did not mention the dragon. Some helpers prefer to stay a secret. He only said, "It was near the well."

That evening Puff hiccuped once, a tiny bell sound, and curled back into the cup. "Good wish," it murmured. "Next week, if you dust gently, I may be awake."

Dev put the cup on the shelf, chip facing the wall so the dragon would not feel stared at. He told his grandmother the kitchen was clean. She looked at the saucer, smiled as if she already knew, and poured her own tea into a different cup.

Before bed Nia sewed the button onto her pocket and declared the star back on duty. Dev listened to the quiet house. From the shelf came a breath of steam and a dream of clouds, small enough to fit in a Sunday, large enough to make a sister glad.

On Monday Dev dusted the shelf with extra care. Puff did not wake, but the chip in the cup caught a little light and looked like a smile. At school Nia showed the button to her friend and said a crow had been reasonable. Dev said nothing about dragons. Secrets are lighter when you do not pass them around like sweets.

That week a neighbor lost a key. Dev almost asked the cup for help, then remembered the rule. He held the thought of the neighbor, not the key, and waited. Puff stayed asleep. So Dev and Nia looked under the doormat the ordinary way and found the key beside a pebble. The wish, it seemed, had already been spent on a sister. Ordinary looking was enough, and it felt like a second kind of magic.

On Sunday Puff opened one eye. "Any large wishes?" it asked. "I am saving my hiccups." Dev shook his head. "Only dusting," he said. The dragon sighed, pleased, and went back to its cloud. Grandmother poured tea in the other cup. The chipped one stayed a house, not a dish, and the kitchen smelled like a story that knew when to be quiet.

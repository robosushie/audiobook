In the corner of a small town there was a garden that woke before the people did. Dew sat on the grass like tiny lamps. A wooden bench, smooth from years of sitting, waited under a neem tree. Birds tried their morning songs, and one bold myna hopped along the bench as if it had booked the seat.

Meera arrived with her schoolbag bumping her side. She was seven, and her pocket was full of fallen blossoms gathered on the way. She liked the garden because it was never in a hurry. The path curved. The fountain coughed once, then found its voice. A dog shook water from its ears and looked proud of the noise.

On the bench sat Mr. Das, who came every morning with a newspaper he rarely opened. His hair was white at the edges, and his shoes were tied in careful bows. He nodded at Meera the way neighbors do when they have shared a place for a long time.

"The bench saved me a seat," Meera said, which was her way of saying hello.

"It saves one for anyone who walks slowly," said Mr. Das.

Meera set the blossoms between them. They were pink and a little crushed, and they smelled like the inside of a warm afternoon. A breeze turned the leaves into soft clapping. Meera listened. She was learning that listening was a kind of looking.

"When I was your age," said Mr. Das, "this garden was smaller, and the bench was new. It complained if you sat down too fast. My sister and I raced paper boats in the drain after rain. We thought the boats were ships, and the drain was a sea with a serious job."

Meera tried to picture a younger Mr. Das with muddy knees. It made her smile. "Did the boats win?"

"One reached the corner. One got stuck under a leaf and decided that was far enough. We called that one the wise boat."

A child laughed beyond the hedge. The myna flew up, thought better of it, and landed again. Meera swung her feet, because they did not yet reach the ground. She told Mr. Das about the story her teacher had read, the one where a seed sleeps all winter and wakes surprised to be a flower. She was not sure seeds could be surprised, but she liked the idea.
